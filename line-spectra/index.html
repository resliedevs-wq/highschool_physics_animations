5.8 Emission and absorption line spectra

hf = E1 − E2 En = −13.6 eVn2 c = fλ
h
Planck constant, 6.63 × 10−34 J s
f
frequency of the photon emitted or absorbed
E1, E2
energies of the higher and lower levels
En
energy of level n in a hydrogen atom
c
speed of light, 3.00 × 108 m s−1
λ
wavelength of the photon

Normally an electron lifted to n = 2 falls back to n = 1 in about a nanosecond, long before another photon can reach it, so cool hydrogen absorbs only ultraviolet. Hydrogen absorbs visible light only when it is hot, as in a star's atmosphere, where collisions keep knocking some electrons up to n = 2.

But in this demonstration an electron lifted to n = 2 stays there until it absorbs a second photon, so the visible lines from n = 2 appear as well. Electrons lifted above n = 2 fall back without absorbing again.
